01 — THE STORY

Ideas can take
a nation further.

Bacharuddin Jusuf Habibie (June 25, 1936 – September 11, 2019) was an Indonesian aerospace engineer who brought technical expertise and industrial vision to the national stage.

Born in Parepare, South Sulawesi, Habibie pursued engineering studies in Germany after beginning his education in Bandung. He earned his doctorate from RWTH Aachen University in 1965 and subsequently built his career in the German aerospace industry. His professional experience shaped his expertise in structural analysis, thermodynamics, and aircraft aerodynamics.

In 1974, Habibie returned to Indonesia to contribute to the development of national technology. He played a leading role in the country's aircraft industry and helped strengthen Indonesia's domestic engineering capabilities. One of the defining milestones of this journey was the development of the N-250 Gatotkaca, an Indonesian-designed passenger aircraft introduced in 1995.

Beyond engineering, Habibie also played a significant role in Indonesian political history. He served as the third President of the Republic of Indonesia from 1998 to 1999, during the country's transition into the Reformasi era. His legacy demonstrates how knowledge, decisive leadership, and dedication can work together to create meaningful progress.
